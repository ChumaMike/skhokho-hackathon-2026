## A. Current staff/auth architecture

- **Three separate concepts already exist and are already partially separated, just not consistently wired together:**
  - `BusinessStaff.staffRole` (`prisma/schema.prisma:372`) — the `StaffRole` enum (`OWNER | MANAGER | STAFF | CASHIER | TECHNICIAN`, `prisma/schema.prisma:80-86`). A label stored per person.
  - `BusinessRole` (`prisma/schema.prisma:337-353`) — a per-business, named, mutable row (`name`, `permissions: Json`, `isDefault`). `BusinessStaff.roleId` (nullable FK) points at one.
  - `StaffPermissions` (`src/types/index.ts:46-70`) — the typed shape of that JSON: 15 boolean flags (`canManageStaff`, `canManageInventory`, `canManageOrders`, `canViewAnalytics`, `canManageCustomers`, `canManageServices`, `canProcessPayments`, `canManageSettings`, `canReadBookings`, `canManageBookings`, `canIssueLoyalty`, `canAdjustLoyalty`, `canInviteStaff`, `canManageDomains`, `canManageBilling`).
- **Every business gets exactly two `BusinessRole` rows, created once, at business creation** (`createBusiness`, `src/modules/tenant/tenant.service.ts:92-128`): an `Owner` role (all 15 flags `true`) and a `Staff` role (`isDefault: true`; `canManageOrders`/`canManageCustomers`/`canProcessPayments`/`canReadBookings`/`canManageBookings`/`canIssueLoyalty` `true`, the other 9 `false`). No other role is ever created unless a business owner later hand-edits `BusinessRole` rows directly in the database — there is no UI anywhere in the app that creates, edits, or lists `BusinessRole` rows. The invite form's "role" picker (see §B) does not create one.
- **Authorization is enforced by `requireStaffPermission(permission, tenantId?)`** (`src/lib/auth/guards.ts:134-169`): resolves the caller's JWT, short-circuits `true` for anyone with `payload.isOwner` (owners bypass every permission check by construction — line 148), otherwise loads the caller's real `BusinessStaff` row + its `role.permissions` JSON and checks the one named flag. This is the single authoritative enforcement point in the codebase — confirmed used correctly (tenant-scoped, session-derived, never trusts client input) across every action file checked (§D below).
- **Page rendering (GET) is not permission-gated the same way actions are.** `requireOwnerSession`/`requireStaffSession` (`src/app/dashboard/session.ts:13-30`) call `requireStaff()`/`requireOwner()` — both check *authentication* and, for the latter, *ownership*, but neither checks a `StaffPermissions` flag. Two pages special-case this by rendering a "you don't have permission" banner in place of content instead of hiding the nav link (`src/app/dashboard/analytics/page.tsx:22-27`, `src/app/dashboard/settings/page.tsx:23-27`, both calling `requireStaffPermission` directly inside the page component, catching the thrown `AuthError` to decide what to render). Every other dashboard page renders its full content to any authenticated team member regardless of their permissions — the mutation, not the view, is where access is actually cut off.
- **`DashboardNav` has no permission or role input at all.** Its only prop is `current: DashboardSection` (`src/components/DashboardNav.tsx:39`); `SECTIONS` (lines 16-31) is a flat, static, 9-item array rendered identically for the owner and every team member. A cashier and the owner see the exact same 9 links.
- **`Order.staffId` and `Appointment.staffId` both already exist** (`prisma/schema.prisma:689-690`, `:757-758` [service/staff relation lines confirmed at `:759-760`]) as nullable FKs to `BusinessStaff`. Confirmed meaning by tracing every write site: `Order.staffId` is set once, at creation, from `CreateOrderSchema.staffId` (`src/modules/orders/order.service.ts:33,60`) — it means **"which staff member rang this order up,"** not "who is fulfilling/delivering it." `Appointment.staffId` (`src/modules/appointments/appointment.service.ts:17`) means **"which staff member is assigned to perform this booking"** — but its own comment (lines 49-56) confirms it is **never actually populated by any real booking today**: the storefront (`src/app/api/store/book/route.ts`) has no staff-selection UI, so `staffId` is always `undefined` on every customer-created appointment, and the service-capacity advisory lock is keyed on `(businessId, serviceId)` only — not staff — meaning "capacity" today models *the service as a whole*, not *a specific person's calendar*. Neither field is a delivery-driver field; no delivery concept exists anywhere in the schema (confirmed — `grep` for "deliver" across `prisma/schema.prisma` finds nothing).
- **`AuditLog` already exists** (`prisma/schema.prisma:506-519`) — generic `actorType`/`actorId`/`businessId`/`action`/`objectType`/`objectId`/`ip`/`createdAt`. Confirmed **write-only from the identity-core RPCs** (`grep` for `audit_log` finds it only in `prisma/sql/10_identity_rpcs.sql` and `identity_layer.sql`; zero references anywhere in `src/`) — this is the **security audit event log** for identity-core actions (consent, cross-business linking), matching this repo's `CLAUDE.md` statement that identity tables are RLS/RPC-managed. It has never been used for, and per the file's own architectural boundary should not be used for, business-operation events (order status changes, staff actions on a booking).
- **`BusinessNotification`** (`prisma/schema.prisma:839-855`) is the only other event-shaped model — but it's an inbox (`type`/`title`/`message`/`isRead`), addressed to the business as a whole, not a structured, ordered, per-actor timeline. It is not a substitute for an operational event log (see §L).
- **No staff availability/schedule model exists at all** — confirmed by `grep` across `prisma/schema.prisma` for "availability": zero matches. A service provider's "own schedule" (vision §2) has nothing to query today beyond "which appointments have my `staffId`," and since that field is never populated (above), there is currently no way to answer "what is Thabo's day" for any real booking.

## B. Confirmed problems

Ranked by concreteness, all confirmed from code in this pass (not assumed):

1. **`StaffRole` (the invite-time label) has zero effect on `BusinessRole` (the actual permission gate) — fully confirmed.** `inviteStaff` (`src/modules/staff/staff.service.ts:89-122`) accepts `validated.staffRole` (one of `MANAGER | STAFF | CASHIER | TECHNICIAN`, `InviteStaffSchema`, line 30) and stores it on `BusinessStaff.staffRole` (line 114) — but the `roleId` it assigns (line 109, `defaultRole?.id`) always comes from `prisma.businessRole.findFirst({ where: { businessId, isDefault: true } })`, i.e. the single `Staff` role created once at business creation (§A) — **completely independent of which `staffRole` the owner picked.** An owner labelling someone "Manager" and someone else "Cashier" produces two `BusinessStaff` rows with identical permissions.
2. **Three of the 15 `StaffPermissions` flags are assigned but never checked anywhere.** `canManageInventory`, `canInviteStaff`, and `canManageStaff` are each set (`true`/`false`) in the two default roles (`tenant.service.ts:98-104`, `:120-126`) and in `ALL_PERMISSIONS`/`NO_PERMISSIONS` (`src/types/index.ts:72-106`), but a repo-wide search for `requireStaffPermission("canManageInventory")`, `"canInviteStaff")`, `"canManageStaff")` in `src/app` returns **zero call sites**. Stock movements are unauthenticated-by-permission (see #4); staff invite/manage is gated by `requireOwner()` instead (`src/app/dashboard/staff/actions.ts:16`), bypassing the permission system entirely — so `canManageStaff`/`canInviteStaff` are dead flags that imply a delegation path ("a trusted non-owner manager could invite staff") the code doesn't actually offer.
3. **Product and service management share one permission flag, `canManageServices`**, with no separate `canManageProducts`. Confirmed: `src/app/dashboard/catalog/actions.ts`'s `permittedRedirect`/`permittedSession` helpers (lines 21-52) are the single gate for all nine exported catalog actions (`addProduct`, `addService`, `editProduct`, `editService`, `toggleProduct`, `toggleService`, `removeProduct`, `removeService`, plus image handling) and both call `requireStaffPermission("canManageServices")` — never `canManageInventory` or any products-specific flag. A role wanting "can edit the menu but not touch bookable services" (a realistic Food-vendor Kitchen/Counter split) cannot be expressed today; the permission is coarser than the vision's per-workspace model needs.
4. **Stock/inventory adjustments have no permission check at all.** `adjustStock` (`src/modules/inventory/inventory.service.ts`, called from `getLowStockItems`'s sibling write paths) accepts an optional `staffId` (line 33) purely for `StockMovement.staffId` attribution — confirmed by reading the full inventory module and its call sites — there is no `requireStaffPermission` call anywhere in that module or its action-layer callers. Combined with #2 (`canManageInventory` unused), stock adjustment is currently reachable by any authenticated staff member with **zero role check** — a real gap for the vision's "Stock" workspace, which explicitly wants adjustments "where authorised."
5. **A pre-existing, unrelated silent-failure bug worth carrying into this design, not fixing here:** `resolveActingStaffId`'s own comment (`src/modules/staff/staff.service.ts:36-52`) documents that `markOrderComplete` has been passing the **owner's `AppUser.id`** (not a real `BusinessStaff.id`) into `completeOrder → adjustStock` "since that code was written," silently failing every stock deduction triggered by an owner completing an order, because `completeOrder` swallows that error deliberately (a stock-deduction failure must never block order completion). `resolveActingStaffId` itself is the fix (resolves an owner token to the owner's real `BusinessStaff` row) and is already used correctly in `src/app/dashboard/orders/actions.ts:32-33` — so this is **already fixed at the call site that matters**, just left as a documented historical note. No action needed from this design; noted for completeness since it touches the same `staffId`-attribution territory as the delivery-assignment work below.
6. **`DashboardNav` shows every section to every authenticated team member** (§A) — a Cashier sees "Staff" (which 403s via `requireOwnerOnlySession` if clicked through to a mutating action, but the page itself may render before failing — needs confirming per-page, see §J) and "Settings"/"Analytics" (which render permission-denied banners in place of content, not a hidden link). This is a UX gap, not a security hole — every mutating action is still gated correctly — but it fails the vision's "navigation should reflect what this person can do" goal as-is.
7. **No staff-to-service or staff-to-availability model exists**, so "a service provider sees their own schedule" (vision Part 2) is not buildable today without new schema — not because of a bug, but because the underlying concept (which staff member(s) can perform which service, and when they're available) was never modeled. `Appointment.staffId` alone is insufficient since it's never populated by the one flow that creates appointments today (§A).
8. **No delivery concept exists anywhere** — not a stub, not a placeholder field, genuinely absent. `Order` has no address, delivery-method, or delivery-fee field; `OrderItem`/`Order` together model *what was bought*, never *how it reaches the customer*. Confirmed by reading the full `Order`/`OrderItem` model block (`prisma/schema.prisma:682-744`) and the storefront checkout route (`src/app/api/store/order/route.ts`), which collects only `businessId`, `productId`, `phone`, `quantity` — no address field is even accepted from the customer today.

## C. Proposed staff domain model

The existing three-layer separation (§A) is already the right shape — **`BusinessStaff` (a person) → `BusinessRole` (a permission set) → `StaffPermissions` (the granular flags)** — it just needs a fourth, currently-missing concept threaded through it, and the *assignment* step (§B.1) fixed. Recommended additions, all additive:

- **Add `jobTitle` as a free-text-with-suggestions field on `BusinessStaff`**, not a growing enum. The existing `StaffRole` enum (`OWNER | MANAGER | STAFF | CASHIER | TECHNICIAN`) becomes **legacy/display-only** — kept for backward compatibility (existing rows keep a value, nothing breaks), but no longer the thing that drives permissions or workspace. A new nullable `String? jobTitle` column (e.g. "Driver", "Kitchen", "Cashier", "Stylist") is what the owner actually types or picks from a preset list at invite time — this is the "flexible job title" the brief asks for, and a plain string can never run out of values the way an enum does.
- **Add a `primaryWorkspace` enum on `BusinessStaff`**, small and closed by design (`OWNER | COUNTER | KITCHEN | DELIVERY | SERVICE_PROVIDER | STOCK | MANAGER | GENERAL`) — this is the one enum that's safe to keep small, because it names *UI surfaces to build*, not *job titles a business might invent*. A workspace enum growing to 8 values is fine; a job-title enum growing to 30 is the anti-pattern the brief correctly warns against. `jobTitle` and `primaryWorkspace` are deliberately two different fields: "Barber" and "Nail Technician" are different job titles that both map to the same `SERVICE_PROVIDER` workspace.
- **`BusinessRole.permissions` stays exactly as it is (a flexible JSON bag)** — no schema change needed here. What changes is *how a role gets assigned*: instead of always picking the one `isDefault: true` row (§B.1), `inviteStaff` should create-or-reuse a `BusinessRole` row matching the chosen preset (see §E) at invite time, the same mechanism `createBusiness` already uses for the Owner/Staff pair — just extended to more presets, on demand, per business, rather than always exactly two.
- **Multiple jobs per person (e.g. Cashier + Driver): do not model this as multiple `BusinessStaff` rows, and do not build a many-to-many role table for MVP.** The brief's own instruction to avoid unnecessary complexity applies directly here. Recommended MVP answer: a single `BusinessStaff` row keeps one `primaryWorkspace` (what their home screen defaults to) and one `BusinessRole` (their permission ceiling), but that role's `permissions` JSON can simply carry the union of both jobs' flags (e.g. Cashier+Driver = `canManageOrders` + `canProcessPayments` + delivery flags all `true` on one role named "Cashier + Driver"). This reuses 100% of the existing single-role architecture and costs nothing new. A genuine many-to-many "staff can switch between multiple named workspaces" feature is real, separable scope — defer it (§Q) until a business actually asks for workspace-*switching* UI, not just combined permissions, which today's model already supports.
- **Multi-location**: out of scope for this pass — `Business` has no location/branch model at all today (confirmed: no `Location`/`Branch` model in the schema), and nothing in the current vision brief requires it yet. Flagging only so a future `BusinessStaff.locationId` doesn't get bolted on without a `Location` model existing first.

## D. Role vs permission vs workspace architecture

Three distinct axes, cleanly separable using what already exists plus the two new `BusinessStaff` columns from §C:

| Axis | Answers | Storage | Change needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Job / title** | "What do we call what they do?" | `BusinessStaff.jobTitle` (new, free string) | New nullable column |
| **Permissions** | "What are they allowed to do?" | `BusinessRole.permissions` (existing JSON) | No schema change — assignment logic changes (§F/§K) |
| **Workspace** | "What do they see when they open the app?" | `BusinessStaff.primaryWorkspace` (new, small closed enum) | New nullable column with a default |

This mirrors the brief's own PERSON → JOB → PERMISSIONS → WORKSPACE chain exactly, using the existing `BusinessStaff`/`BusinessRole` pair as the PERSON/PERMISSIONS layers and adding JOB (`jobTitle`) and WORKSPACE (`primaryWorkspace`) as two small, independent, nullable additions — neither one replaces or migrates the other. **Server-side authorization is decided from `BusinessRole.permissions` alone, always** — `primaryWorkspace` only ever decides *which components render*, never *what a request is allowed to do* (see §J's "hiding a nav item must never replace authorization," which the existing `requireStaffPermission` architecture already satisfies for every checked action — the gap is only in the unchecked ones from §B).

## E. Recommended role presets

Seven presets, matching the brief's own list, each a **named `BusinessRole` template** (a `permissions` JSON shape + a suggested default `primaryWorkspace`) that `inviteStaff` instantiates per-business on first use — not a fixed global table, since `BusinessRole` is already per-business (allows an owner to later hand-edit one business's "Cashier" role without affecting any other business, exactly as the model already supports):

| Preset | Suggested `primaryWorkspace` | Permissions granted (existing `StaffPermissions` flags) | New flags needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Manager** | `MANAGER` | `canManageOrders`, `canManageBookings`, `canReadBookings`, `canManageCustomers`, `canProcessPayments`, `canIssueLoyalty`, `canViewAnalytics`, `canManageInventory` (finally enforced, §I) | none |
| **Counter / Cashier** | `COUNTER` | `canManageOrders`, `canManageCustomers`, `canProcessPayments`, `canIssueLoyalty`, `canReadBookings` | none — matches today's existing default `Staff` role almost exactly |
| **Kitchen / Preparation** | `KITCHEN` | `canManageOrders` (scoped in UI to preparation-state transitions only, §F) | none — reuses `canManageOrders`; the *workspace* component restricts which fields/actions render, permission stays coarse |
| **Driver** | `DELIVERY` | new `canManageDeliveries` (see §I) | **1 new flag**: `canManageDeliveries` |
| **Service Provider** | `SERVICE_PROVIDER` | `canManageBookings`, `canReadBookings` (UI scoped to "my own" appointments, §F) | none |
| **Stock** | `STOCK` | `canManageInventory` (finally enforced, §I) | none |
| **General Staff** | `GENERAL` | today's existing default `Staff` role, unchanged (`canManageOrders`/`canManageCustomers`/`canProcessPayments`/`canReadBookings`/`canManageBookings`/`canIssueLoyalty`) | none |

Only **one new permission flag is genuinely required** (`canManageDeliveries`) — everything else is a *recombination* of the 15 flags that already exist, finally assigned correctly per preset instead of collapsed into one "Staff" default for everyone. This directly answers the brief's "avoid an ever-growing enum": the enum that would have grown (job titles) is replaced by free text (§C); the thing that stays closed and small (workspaces) tops out at 8 values; permissions stay a flat, addable-to JSON bag, exactly as already built.

## F. Workspace UX for each preset

All eight (`OWNER` plus the seven from §E) are **presentation-layer filters over data the app already fetches**, following the exact pattern this session's own `NeedsAttention`/`QuickActions`/`ComingUp` dashboard-home components already established (`src/app/dashboard/page.tsx`, `NeedsAttention.tsx`, `QuickActions.tsx`, `ComingUp.tsx` — all built from existing service calls, no new queries) — the same reuse-first mechanism, applied per-workspace instead of per-business-category:

- **Owner** — today's full dashboard, unchanged.
- **Manager** — today's full dashboard minus billing/domains/staff-invite (already permission-gated correctly at the action level per §A; workspace-level change is only which nav links render).
- **Counter/Cashier** — a trimmed home: customer lookup + stamp (already built, `stampByPhone`/`stampMember`, `src/app/dashboard/customers/actions.ts`), active/new orders (`listOrders`), no analytics/settings/staff links. Everything needed already exists as a service call; this is a new, smaller nav array + a new landing page composed from existing components, not new backend logic.
- **Kitchen/Preparation** — a *new* focused view, not a trim of an existing one: needs the order-pipeline transition actions from the gap-analysis's §I.2 (`PENDING→CONFIRMED→PROCESSING→READY`) to exist first, since there's currently only `markOrderComplete`. Once those exist, the Kitchen workspace is a filtered `listOrders` view (status ∈ {CONFIRMED, PROCESSING}) with only "Start preparing"/"Mark ready" actions — reuses `OrderItem.name`/`quantity` (already selected fields), explicitly should NOT select `Order.membership` phone/contact fields into this view (§M).
- **Driver/Delivery** — genuinely new UI *and* new backend (§G) — cannot be built as a filtered view of anything that exists today, since no delivery data exists to filter.
- **Service Provider** — needs `Appointment.staffId` to actually be populated (§B.7/§I) before "my day" can mean anything; once populated, it's `listUpcomingAppointments` filtered by `staffId = self`, which the service function's existing signature already supports as an optional filter shape (confirmed the query is a straightforward `where` builder, `appointment.service.ts`).
- **Stock** — a filtered view over `InventoryItem`/`StockMovement`, both of which already have full CRUD services (`inventory.service.ts`) with zero permission gate today (§B.4) — the workspace and the permission-fix land together.
- **General Staff** — today's existing default experience, unchanged; the fallback for any business that doesn't want to bother with presets yet.

## G. Delivery architecture

**A new `OrderDelivery` model is warranted, but only once merchant-initiated fulfillment tracking is actually wanted — not required to close any currently-broken UI, since no delivery UI exists to be broken.** Recommend treating this as clearly separable, later-P1/P2 scope (§S), not bundled with the P0 role/permission fix. Design, so it doesn't block anything else architecturally:

- **Do not overload `Order.staffId`.** Confirmed meaning (§A): "who rang this order up," set once at creation. A driver is a *distinct* actor assigned *after* creation, potentially changing (reassignment), with its own state machine — a second FK on `Order` (`Order.driverStaffId`) would conflate two unrelated facts and couldn't cleanly express "unassigned" vs "assigned-but-not-yet-accepted" vs history. A separate model is the right call, matching the brief's own instinct.
- **Recommended `OrderDelivery` fields** (evaluating the brief's proposed list against what's genuinely needed for MVP):
  - **Keep:** `id`, `businessId` (denormalized for query scoping, matching every other tenant-scoped model's pattern), `orderId` (unique — one delivery per order, MVP doesn't need split-shipment), `driverStaffId` (nullable — unassigned state), `status`, `deliveryAddressSnapshot` (a plain string or small JSON, captured **at order time**, not joined live to a customer profile — see next bullet), `deliveryInstructions` (nullable string), `assignedAt`, `pickedUpAt`, `deliveredAt`, `failedAt`, `failureReason` (nullable string).
  - **Defer, don't build yet:** `acceptedAt`/`enRouteAt` as separate timestamps — collapsing "assigned → accepted → picked up" into fewer states is likely sufficient for MVP (see §H); `proofOfDelivery` (a photo/signature capture is a real feature with its own upload-and-review UX, not a field to add speculatively); `deliveryFee` — only add this when checkout actually calculates one, otherwise it's a column nothing ever writes, repeating the exact `operatingHours`/`accentColor` dead-field pattern the gap-analysis already flagged twice (its §E.8, §E.9).
  - **Snapshot, don't reference live:** the brief's own concern — "the original customer delivery details must be preserved even if the customer later changes their profile" — is real and already has a precedent in this codebase: `OrderItem.name`/`unitPrice` are already snapshotted at order time rather than joined live to `Product` (confirmed, `prisma/schema.prisma:734-739`, `order.service.ts:81` copies `item.name`/`item.unitPrice` at creation), specifically so a later product-price edit never rewrites history. `deliveryAddressSnapshot` should follow the identical pattern — a plain string/JSON column on `OrderDelivery`, never a live FK to a mutable "customer address" record (which doesn't exist today anyway — `Membership` carries no address field, confirmed).
- **The storefront/Order model does not currently capture a delivery address or method at all** (§B.8) — this is a genuine prerequisite, not something `OrderDelivery` alone fixes. `CreateOrderSchema` (`order.service.ts:31-43`) would need a new optional `deliveryMethod: 'COLLECTION' | 'DELIVERY'` and, when `DELIVERY`, an address string — small, additive, but real new checkout-flow work, sequenced before `OrderDelivery` can be populated by anything.
- **Dispatch strategies — both are safe to design for now, build one first:** MANAGER ASSIGN is the simpler MVP (a manager picks a driver from `listStaff` filtered to `primaryWorkspace = DELIVERY`, an ordinary permission-gated update) — build this first. DRIVER CLAIM needs real concurrency protection: **recommend the same advisory-lock pattern already used twice in this codebase** (`appointment.service.ts`'s `pg_advisory_xact_lock(hashtextextended(...))` for booking capacity, `prisma/sql/10_identity_rpcs.sql` for `register_customer`/`redeem_reward`) — a claim is `UPDATE order_deliveries SET driver_staff_id = $1, status = 'ASSIGNED' WHERE id = $2 AND status = 'UNASSIGNED'` inside a transaction guarded by an advisory lock keyed on the delivery id, or more simply, Prisma's own optimistic pattern: `updateMany({ where: { id, status: 'UNASSIGNED' }, data: { driverStaffId, status: 'ASSIGNED' } })` and checking the returned `count === 1` — a plain conditional `UPDATE ... WHERE status = 'UNASSIGNED'` is already atomic in Postgres without needing the advisory-lock machinery at all, since there's no read-then-write gap to race (unlike the booking-capacity case, which has to count first). **Recommend the conditional-`updateMany` approach — it's simpler than the existing advisory-lock pattern and correctly race-free for this specific case**, reserving advisory locks for cases (like booking capacity) that genuinely need a read+decision before the write.

## H. Delivery/order state machines

**Order pipeline** (already scoped by the gap-analysis, restated for completeness): `PENDING → CONFIRMED → PROCESSING → READY → COMPLETED`, with `CANCELLED` reachable from any pre-`COMPLETED` state. All six `OrderStatus` enum values already exist in the schema (`prisma/schema.prisma:88-96`, including `REFUNDED`, not yet wired to any UI — out of scope here, belongs with payment/refund work).

**Delivery status — recommend collapsing the brief's 8 proposed states to 5 for MVP**, explicitly challenging the longer list as unnecessary complexity the brief itself invited scrutiny of:

- `UNASSIGNED → ASSIGNED → PICKED_UP → DELIVERED`, plus `FAILED` (reachable from `ASSIGNED` or `PICKED_UP`).
- **Drop `ACCEPTED` as a separate state from `ASSIGNED` for MVP**: the practical difference between "a manager assigned this to Sizwe" and "Sizwe has acknowledged it" only matters once accept/decline UI exists — until then, `ASSIGNED` alone is sufficient and `assignedAt` already timestamps the moment; add `ACCEPTED`/`acceptedAt` back the moment decline becomes a real feature, since it's an additive enum value + one nullable timestamp, not a redesign.
- **Drop `EN_ROUTE` as a separate state from `PICKED_UP` for MVP**: without live GPS (explicitly out of scope per the brief), "en route" carries no information a driver-facing app can act on differently than "picked up" — it's a status word with no behavior attached yet. Add it back only alongside actual live-tracking work, which the brief already says to design for without building now; a 5-state machine growing to 6 later is a trivial migration (append an enum value), not a structural risk.
- **Drop `CANCELLED` as a delivery-specific state**: an order-level cancellation (`OrderStatus.CANCELLED`) should cascade to the delivery via application logic (mark any in-flight `OrderDelivery` `FAILED` with a system-generated `failureReason`), not duplicate the concept at both levels.

This keeps the new enum small and matches the same "don't add a state nothing reads yet" discipline the gap-analysis already applied to `ORDER_STATUS_LABELS` having entries with no transition (its §E.3) — the fix here is to not repeat that mistake in the new model.

## I. Business capability model

**The existing `Business.settings: Json?` column (`prisma/schema.prisma:203`) is sufficient — no new table or framework needed.** Confirmed currently unused for this purpose (a repo-wide read of every `Business.settings` reference shows it referenced only by `UpdateBusinessSchema` as an accepted-but-unwritten passthrough, matching the same "schema-only" pattern already flagged for `operatingHours` in the gap-analysis §B.5/§E.8) — meaning it's free to use for exactly this without any migration:

- Store `capabilities: string[]` inside `Business.settings` JSON (e.g. `{ capabilities: ["ORDERS", "PRODUCTS", "PICKUP", "DELIVERY", "LOYALTY"] }`), defaulted from `BusinessCategory` at creation time via a small static lookup table (`BusinessCategory → default capability list`) — the exact same "static config keyed by category" mechanism the existing `velvety-honking-otter.md` plan's §6 already specifies for dashboard card personalization, reused here for a second purpose rather than inventing a second mechanism.
- **The one genuinely new permission flag from §E, `canManageDeliveries`, should be read together with the `DELIVERY` capability** — a business without `DELIVERY` in its capability list simply never shows delivery-related workspace UI or the Driver preset as an invite option, regardless of what any staff member's permissions say. Capabilities gate *what the business offers*; permissions gate *what a person can do within what's offered*. Keeping these as two separate concerns (business-level list vs. person-level flags) avoids the trap of trying to express "this business doesn't do delivery" as a permission, which it isn't.
- Explicitly **not recommended**: a dedicated `BusinessCapability` join table, a capability-rules engine, or per-capability feature-flag service — all would be solving a problem the existing nullable JSON column plus a static lookup already solves at zero migration cost. Revisit only if capabilities ever need to be independently queried/filtered at the database level (e.g., "find all businesses with DELIVERY enabled" for a future ops dashboard) — `Json` can't be indexed as cheaply as a real column for that, but nothing in the current brief needs that query.

## J. Navigation/authorization architecture

**Confirmed finding, direct answer to the brief's explicit question:** navigation visibility and server-side authorization are already architecturally separate in this codebase (`DashboardNav` has zero auth logic; `requireStaffPermission` has zero rendering logic) — they just aren't *connected* yet, since `DashboardNav` takes no permission input at all (§A). This is the right starting architecture, not a flaw to redesign — it needs wiring, not replacing.

- **Recommend a single new function, `getWorkspaceNav(session)`**, colocated with `DashboardNav.tsx` or in a new `src/lib/workspace.ts`, that takes the resolved `StaffPermissions` (already available at every page via `requireStaffPermission`/`requireStaff`) and the new `primaryWorkspace` (§C), and returns the filtered `SECTIONS` array plus which section should be the default landing page. This is the "central workspace/navigation policy" the brief asks for — one function, one place, rather than scattering `if (permission)` checks across every page's own render logic.
- **Hard rule to preserve, already true today and must remain true:** navigation filtering is additive convenience only. Every existing `requireStaffPermission` call site in every action file remains exactly as strict as it is today — hiding "Staff" from a Cashier's nav is a UX improvement, not a substitute for `requireOwner()` already gating the invite action itself. No proposed change here loosens or replaces any existing server-side check.
- **The one real, currently-unaddressed gap this surfaces (already noted in §B.6):** because page-level GET requests aren't uniformly permission-checked, a team member who navigates directly to a URL not in their filtered nav (e.g. a Cashier typing `/dashboard/staff` in the address bar) may see a page shell before any mutation attempt fails. **Recommend closing this at the page level for the pages that currently fully render regardless of permission** — `src/app/dashboard/staff/page.tsx` in particular, since it's owner-only functionality (`requireOwnerOnlySession`, confirmed pattern from `session.ts:23-30`) that should redirect on GET, not just on submit; `analytics`/`settings` already do the right thing (banner-in-place-of-content) and don't need to change. This is a small, targeted list — not a rearchitecture — once §C/§E's presets exist and give every non-owner a real `primaryWorkspace` to check against.

## K. Staff onboarding

The brief's "What does this person do?" → preset → human-readable summary → "Customise access" flow is buildable entirely from what §C-§E already define, as new UI in `src/app/dashboard/staff/page.tsx`'s invite form plus one new server action:

1. Replace the current `staffRole` `<select>` (`MANAGER | STAFF | CASHIER | TECHNICIAN`, `InviteStaffSchema`) with a preset picker over the 7 options from §E (labels only — "Manager", "Counter/Cashier", "Kitchen/Preparation", "Driver", "Service Provider", "Stock", "General Staff").
2. On selection, render the already-known permission set as a static "✓/✕" summary (no new data needed — it's the preset's fixed `StaffPermissions` object from §E, rendered client-side before submission).
3. `inviteStaff` (§C) resolves the chosen preset to a `BusinessRole` row — create-if-missing per business (first time a business invites a "Driver," a `BusinessRole` named "Driver" is created for that business; a second Driver invite reuses it) — and sets `primaryWorkspace` from the same preset.
4. "Customise access" is a **deferred, separate feature** — a per-role permission editor over the existing `BusinessRole.permissions` JSON, which needs its own small UI (checkboxes over the 16 flags including the new `canManageDeliveries`) but no new schema; scope it only once the preset flow above ships and an owner actually asks for finer control, per the brief's own "don't force RBAC concepts on day one" instruction.

## L. Audit/accountability design

**Two genuinely different things, correctly kept apart, matching what the codebase already does architecturally for a different pair of concerns:**

- **Security audit events** (who linked/consented/accessed identity-core data) — **already exists, already correctly scoped, do not touch.** `AuditLog` (§A) stays RPC-written-only, identity-core-only, exactly as `CLAUDE.md`'s hard rule implies. Nothing in this design should write business-operation events into this table.
- **Business operation events** (order status changes, delivery assignment, staff actions on an order/booking) — **genuinely does not exist today** (§A/§B — `BusinessNotification` is an inbox, not a timeline). Recommend a new, narrowly-scoped `OrderEvent` model (or a shared `OperationEvent` if appointments need the identical shape — evaluate once built whether the two are similar enough to share a table, don't presuppose it): `id`, `orderId` (or a polymorphic `subjectType`/`subjectId` pair if shared), `businessId`, `actorStaffId` (nullable — a system-generated event like auto-cancellation has no actor), `eventType` (string, not enum — `"created" | "status_changed" | "assigned" | "picked_up" | "delivered" | ...`, deliberately a string so new event types don't need a migration, same reasoning as `BusinessNotification.type` already being a plain string, not an enum), `fromStatus`/`toStatus` (nullable, only populated for status-change events), `metadata: Json?`, `createdAt`. **Explicitly reject a single shared "do everything" event table across orders, appointments, deliveries, refunds, and staff actions** — the brief's own instruction ("do not create one giant miscellaneous logging table without reasoning about its purpose") is correct; scope this to order lifecycle first (since that's what §G/§H need to be traceable), and only extend to appointments/staff actions once there's a concrete UI (a "history" tab) that needs to read them, avoiding a write-only table nobody queries.
- **Recording, at minimum, for order status changes and delivery assignment**: every transition in §H, tagged with `actorStaffId` — this is what makes "09:15 Accepted by Lerato... 09:34 Picked up by Sizwe" (the brief's own example) renderable, and it's a straightforward one-row-per-transition insert alongside each new action from §F/§G, not a separate subsystem.
- **Refunds, discounts, loyalty adjustment, payment changes**: **defer building event recording for these until the actions themselves exist.** `Order.discount`/`discountCode` are already schema fields (`prisma/schema.prisma:697-698`) but no discount-application UI exists yet (confirmed: no action anywhere sets them outside `createOrder`'s initial value); `redeemMember`/`stampMember` (loyalty) already write to the append-only `Visit` ledger (`customer.service.ts`, referenced throughout this session), which **already is** an event log for loyalty — no new model needed there, it already exists and already does this correctly.

## M. Privacy/POPIA considerations

- **No new risk to the identity core** — every workspace proposed in §F reads from `businessId`/`membershipId`-scoped queries exactly as today's code already does; nothing here touches `Customer`/`CustomerIdentifier`/`Consent` or crosses the RLS/RPC boundary.
- **Least-privilege data shaping is a `select` clause change, not an access-control change** — the Kitchen workspace (§F) should query orders with an explicit `select` that omits `membership.phone`/`membership.address`-shaped fields (note: `Membership` today carries no address field at all, confirmed — only `publicId`/`shortCode`/`displayName`, so this is actually a smaller concern than the brief anticipated for the Kitchen case specifically) — but the same discipline matters more for the **Driver** workspace, which genuinely does need a delivery address and a contact mechanism (§G) and must not additionally expose loyalty-stamp history or analytics, which the existing `requireStaffPermission("canManageDeliveries")` boundary already prevents at the *action* level — the `select` shaping is the remaining, presentation-layer half of least-privilege, and should be applied per-workspace query, not left to "the component just doesn't render that field" (a field fetched-but-not-rendered is still a data-exposure risk if the response payload is ever inspected).
- **If future location tracking is added**: this design deliberately keeps `OrderDelivery` free of any location/GPS field (§G) precisely so that a future, explicitly-scoped `DeliveryLocationPing` (or similar) table can be added additively, with its own retention policy and explicit staff disclosure, without touching `OrderDelivery`'s core fields — matching the brief's own "design so live tracking can be added later without restructuring."

## N. Data/schema changes

All additive; no destructive change, no enum value removal, no existing column altered:

1. `BusinessStaff.jobTitle String?` — new nullable column (§C).
2. `BusinessStaff.primaryWorkspace` — new enum `StaffWorkspace { OWNER COUNTER KITCHEN DELIVERY SERVICE_PROVIDER STOCK MANAGER GENERAL }`, nullable or defaulted to `GENERAL` (§C).
3. `StaffPermissions.canManageDeliveries: boolean` — one new flag in the existing TypeScript interface + a one-off backfill of existing `BusinessRole.permissions` JSON rows, following the **exact precedent already set** by the Phase 5 backfill (`src/types/index.ts:55-62`'s own comment, `prisma/scripts/backfill-permission-keys.ts`) — a JSON key absent on old rows reads `undefined`/falsy at every `requireStaffPermission` call site, fails closed by construction, no migration required for existing rows to remain valid (§E).
4. New `OrderDelivery` model (§G) — new table, FK to `Order` (unique) and nullable FK to `BusinessStaff`.
5. New `Order.deliveryMethod`/delivery-address capture on the checkout path (§G) — either a new enum column on `Order` or `Order.metadata` JSON reuse; **recommend a real column** (`deliveryMethod DeliveryMethod?`) over stuffing it into the existing untyped `metadata` field, since it needs to be queried/filtered on (e.g., "show only delivery orders" in the Driver workspace) — `Json` fields are the wrong choice for anything that needs a `WHERE` clause, consistent with §I's reasoning for why capabilities are fine in JSON (never filtered at the DB level) while this is not.
6. New `OrderEvent` model (§L) — new table, FK to `Order`, nullable FK to `BusinessStaff`.
7. `Business.settings` JSON gains a `capabilities` key (§I) — no schema change, a new *convention* within an existing column.

## O. Existing fields/models to preserve

Explicitly confirmed sound and reused as-is by this design — do not touch:

- `BusinessStaff`, `BusinessRole`, `StaffPermissions`, `requireStaffPermission` — the entire existing three-layer permission architecture (§A) is the foundation this design extends, not replaces.
- `Order.staffId` — keep its existing "who rang this up" meaning exactly as-is; never repurposed for delivery (§G).
- `AuditLog` and its RPC-only write boundary — untouched, kept identity-core-exclusive (§L).
- `Visit` (loyalty ledger) — already the correct event-log pattern for loyalty; not replaced by the new `OrderEvent` model (§L).
- `OrderItem`'s snapshot-at-creation pattern (`name`/`unitPrice` copied, not live-joined) — the direct precedent `OrderDelivery.deliveryAddressSnapshot` follows (§G).
- `Service.maxBookings` + the advisory-lock capacity mechanism in `appointment.service.ts` — correct and tested (concurrency tests confirmed in the earlier gap-analysis pass); the Service Provider workspace (§F) reads `Appointment.staffId` alongside this, doesn't change how capacity is enforced.
- The `createBusiness`-time pattern of creating named `BusinessRole` rows inside the same transaction as the business itself — the mechanism §E's presets reuse for on-demand role creation, not a new pattern.

## P. Fields/models that should NOT be reused

- **`Order.staffId` must never be repurposed to mean "assigned driver."** Confirmed reason in §A/§G — it already has a distinct, real meaning ("who rang this order up") that predates and is unrelated to fulfillment/delivery.
- **`StaffRole` enum should not gain new values** (`DRIVER`, `KITCHEN`, etc.) — this is the exact anti-pattern the brief warned against; `jobTitle` (free string) and `primaryWorkspace` (the one small closed enum actually worth having) replace the need for `StaffRole` to grow. `StaffRole` itself becomes legacy/display-only (§C) rather than deleted, since deleting it would be a breaking schema change for zero benefit — existing rows keep whatever value they have, nothing reads it for authorization once this ships.
- **`AuditLog` should not be reused for business-operation events** (§L) — it's identity-core-scoped by design and RPC-written-only; mixing business events into it would blur exactly the boundary `CLAUDE.md` draws.
- **`BusinessNotification` should not be stretched into an event timeline** — it's correctly shaped as an inbox (unread/read state, addressed to the business) and shouldn't grow ordering/actor/before-after semantics that belong in the new `OrderEvent` model instead.
- **`Business.settings`/`Order.metadata` (untyped `Json?` columns) should not absorb the delivery-method/address fields** — per §N.5, anything that needs to be queried or filtered on belongs in a real, typed column, not the generic JSON catch-all, even though that catch-all is the right home for `capabilities` (§I), which is never filtered at the database level.

## Q. Migration/backward-compatibility plan

Every item in §N is purely additive — no existing row becomes invalid, no existing query breaks:

- **§N.1-2 (`jobTitle`, `primaryWorkspace`)**: nullable columns, zero backfill required — every existing `BusinessStaff` row simply has both `null` until an owner re-invites or edits them; `primaryWorkspace: null` should be treated as `GENERAL` by every read site (a coding convention, not a DB default, so it's explicit at each call site rather than silently defaulted).
- **§N.3 (`canManageDeliveries`)**: additive JSON key, same backfill mechanism already proven safe once (`prisma/scripts/backfill-permission-keys.ts`) — extend that script or write a twin for this one flag, run once against existing `BusinessRole` rows to explicitly set `false` (fail-closed) rather than leaving it implicitly `undefined` forever.
- **§N.4, §N.6 (`OrderDelivery`, `OrderEvent`)**: brand-new tables, no migration risk beyond the table creation itself — no existing table's shape changes.
- **§N.5 (delivery method/address on `Order`)**: nullable new column(s) on an existing high-traffic table (`orders`) — the one item here worth flagging for careful migration sequencing (add-column-nullable is safe and fast on Postgres, no table rewrite, but should still run during a low-traffic window per this repo's general migration discipline, matching how every prior migration this session was applied via a manually-authored SQL file + `prisma migrate deploy`, never `prisma migrate dev`).
- **Multiple-jobs-per-person (§C's deferred item)**: explicitly requires no migration to defer — the single-role-with-combined-permissions approach uses only what's already being added in §N.1-3; a future many-to-many table is a clean additive change whenever actually needed, not something today's design paints itself into a corner over.
- **`StaffRole` enum**: no migration — kept as-is, reads become advisory/display-only in application code, not removed from the schema.

## S. P0/P1/P2 implementation order

Sequenced to land the cheapest, highest-leverage fix first and treat delivery as later, separable scope — consistent with the parallel gap-analysis's own P0/P1/P2/P3 structure, slotting into it rather than replacing it:

**P0 — fix what's already broken, no new schema beyond two nullable columns and one permission flag:**
1. Fix `inviteStaff` to actually assign a role matching the chosen preset instead of always the single default (§B.1, §C, §K) — the single highest-leverage item in this document, same "small file, big impact" shape as the join-flow session-cookie fix already identified in the parallel gap-analysis.
2. Add `BusinessStaff.jobTitle`/`primaryWorkspace` columns and wire `getWorkspaceNav` (§J) into `DashboardNav`.
3. Enforce `canManageInventory` at the actual inventory-adjustment call sites (§B.4) — closes a real, currently-open authorization gap, not just a UX one.
4. Add `canManageDeliveries` to `StaffPermissions` (schema/type only — no delivery UI yet); harmless to add early since unused flags fail closed.

**P1 — new operating experience, once P0 role/workspace plumbing exists:**
5. Build the Counter/Cashier, Kitchen, Stock, and Service Provider workspace UIs (§F) — all filtered views over existing data, contingent on the order-pipeline transition actions the parallel gap-analysis already scopes as its own P0 item (§I.2 there).
6. Add delivery-method/address capture to checkout (§G, §N.5) — prerequisite for delivery to mean anything.
7. Build `OrderDelivery` + MANAGER ASSIGN dispatch (§G) — ship the simpler dispatch strategy first.
8. Build the `OrderEvent` model + order-lifecycle event recording (§L), since P1's new workspaces are exactly what first makes a "history" view worth having.

**P2 — scale/refinement:**
9. DRIVER CLAIM dispatch strategy (§G) — once MANAGER ASSIGN is proven, add the concurrency-safe claim path.
10. "Customise access" permission editor (§K) — once real businesses have used presets long enough to ask for it.
11. Manager-approval workflow for refunds/discounts/loyalty adjustments (brief's Part 5) — explicitly not designed in detail here per the brief's own instruction not to over-build architecture for it now; the existing `StaffPermissions` JSON shape is already extensible enough (an `ALLOWED`/`APPROVAL_REQUIRED` concept could later be a per-permission enum instead of boolean, a change that's additive to the type, not the storage model) to not be blocked later by anything decided in this document.
12. `EN_ROUTE`/`ACCEPTED` delivery states and live-location tracking (§H, §M) — explicitly deferred, explicitly designed not to require restructuring when added.
